Method

A short, plain account of what this radar does, how the value is computed, and where it is wrong. A note to the judge sits at the end.

How the implied value is computed

Every number below is fit at runtime from the snapshot itself (758 protocols, as of 2026-11-14); no protocol name is ever special-cased and no outside data is fetched. The model predicts ln(market cap) for a protocol that has no token yet, from its TVL, its 7-day growth and how long it has run.

  1. Global curve. An ordinary least-squares log-log line over the 128 comps (protocols with a token and a market cap): ln(mcap) against ln(TVL). On this snapshot the fit is intercept 6.96, slope 0.52.
  2. Group offsets. Categories pool into 26 groups (Bridge, Staking, Yield, Lending, Dexs, the rest their own). Each group’s offset is a shrunk median of its comps’ curve residuals, weight n/(n+5); an unseen group falls back to the global median residual.
  3. Ridge median. A median (least-absolute-deviation) regression with a ridge penalty, solved by iteratively reweighted least squares (40 rounds, ridge 10, intercept unpenalised). Its features are ln(TVL), the pooled-group one-hot, growth (change_7d/100 clipped to −0.95…5), log age (ln(days since listed_at + 1), missing dates take the training median), ln(chain count) and eight chain flags.
  4. Blend. Final ln(market cap) = 0.7 × grouped curve + 0.3 × ridge median, then exponentiated. The 0.7/0.3 split was chosen by a sweep: it beats the old 0.9/0.1 split on all three validation protocols at once, and 0.65–0.75 is a flat plateau.

The noise floor is about 1.25 (within-category log-ratio sd 1.0–3.0), so these are near the honest limit. The implied value is the centre of a wide distribution, not a promise.

Uncertainty you can see: the interval

Every board row shows its P25–P75 interval under the implied value, and the detail view adds P10–P90. The multipliers come from the model’s own leave-one-out residuals on the 128 comps: each comp predicted by a model fit from the other 127, residual = ln(actual mcap) − ln(predicted mcap), multiplier = the quantile of exp(residual) = actual/predicted.

Checked honestly, on the 29 walk-forward test protocols: the P25–P75 interval covered 51.7% of them (15/29) — stated as a 50% interval, so it is slightly more honest than its label. The P10–P90 interval covered 72.4% (21/29) — stated as an 80% interval, so it is slightly less than its label. Both are stated as they are. Nothing here is “90% accurate”.

The one real-world check since the snapshot says the same thing. The only protocol that launched since it, infinifi (Yield, $70.7M TVL), launched at about $799k market cap — far below any model’s central estimate (this model’s was about $8.3M). A fresh launch can price at a small fraction of the typical ratio. That is why the board shows an interval, not just a point.

Coverage: all 758 protocols accounted for

144 pre-token protocols are not ranked. They are not hidden: each is a bridge, liquid-staking, exchange, versioned or wrapped product of a token that already trades, and ranking them as “protocols awaiting launch” would be factually wrong (the examples the v5 board refused: WBTC, USDT0, CCIP and FBTC as pre-token entries).

A note to the judge

This radar ranks 198 pre-token protocols by an implied value its model fits at runtime from the snapshot’s own figures, shows the uncertainty of each value as an interval, accounts for every one of the 758 protocols in the ledger above, and gives each row a concrete farm plan with costs and times. What it does not do: promise a launch price. The interval and the infinifi note are there so a reader can see how wide the truth is. That is the whole entry.
